Description and outcomes

Catalog description

Topics include: E-R model, relational database design, normalization theory, SQL, application design and development, and database administration. Query optimization and concurrent operation issues will be presented. Significant application-oriented projects will be required.

What you will be able to do

On completing the course, every student will be able to:

  1. Use a relational model to design databases (weeks 1–2 and 6, project milestones 1–2).
  2. Employ normalization rules as needed to obtain data in normal form (week 7, Lab 5, milestone 2).
  3. Include security and integrity rules in a database (weeks 5, 9 and 13, Labs 4, 6 and 8, milestone 3).
  4. Write and test a database using SQL (weeks 3–5, Labs 1–4, milestones 2–4).
  5. Proceed with further study in the database area (weeks 10–15, Labs 7–8, milestone 4).

Graduate students (CS 5200) will also be able to:

  1. Understand the differences between NoSQL and SQL databases (week 15, the final exam's NoSQL part).
  2. Use a NoSQL database tool for application development (Lab 9, and the NoSQL feature of the term project).

Every handout marks graduate-only work with Graduate (CS 5200).

The lecture is the basic mechanism of the course, with live demonstrations on the course database. Assessment is criterion-referenced: each piece of work has a rubric, and your grade depends on meeting it, not on how others do.

Grade scale

A 90–100 · B 80–89 · C 70–79 · D 60–69 · F below 60, the scale in the department's master syllabus.

Late work

Labs and project milestones lose 10% per day for up to three days, and are not accepted after that. Quizzes and exams have fixed windows and cannot be late; the online section takes each quiz within 48 hours of the recording. The milestone 4 demo and walkthrough can't be late either: if you can't come on April 27, tell me beforehand. If something makes a deadline impossible, write to me before it passes.

Examinations

  • Midterm: Tuesday, March 2, in class, 90 minutes, 100 points, weeks 1–7. One page of your own handwritten notes, both sides. The week 8 slides show the parts.
  • Final: finals week, May 3–7, at the time in the university's final exam schedule. Comprehensive, 2 hours, 100 points. Two pages of your own handwritten notes and a basic calculator.
  • CS 5200 students answer one extra 10-point question on each exam.
  • The online section takes both exams in Brightspace, with the same time limit.
  • If illness or an emergency makes an exam date impossible, tell me before the exam if you possibly can. A make-up is given for a documented reason.

Course policies

Attendance

Face-to-face students are expected at every Tuesday class, and online students to watch each week's recording before that week's quiz. Attendance is not graded, but the quizzes and exams assume everything shown in class, and the in-class part of Lab 0 gets PostgreSQL working on your computer. If you miss a class, watch its recording. If you will miss an exam, see Examinations.

Communication

There is no discussion board in this course. Ask about the material in class or in office hours. Everything else, including a lab you are stuck on, accommodations, illness, extensions and grading questions, goes to me by email at kar@ucmo.edu.

Labs and the project

The project is individual or a team of two. You may discuss ideas with classmates, but the SQL, code and writing you submit must be your own, and you cite anything you did not write. You use your own computer; a student whose computer can't install software tells me in the first two weeks.
