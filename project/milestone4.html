Part A: indexes, with proof

Work on the data you loaded for milestone 3, at least 1,000 rows in your busiest table. Run ANALYZE first, so the planner knows what you loaded. Weeks 10 to 12 and Lab 7 are the model.

  1. Choose three queries your program actually runs: the ones a user waits for most, or runs most often. Include at least one that looks up a few rows and one report over many.
  2. Measure each one before, with the values your program would pass: 
    EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS, COSTS OFF, TIMING OFF)
SELECT …;
    Note the plan's top steps and its buffers.
  3. Add the indexes that help, at least two in all, with CREATE INDEX, and measure each query again. A composite index, a partial index or an index on an expression counts, if your query needs it.
  4. Keep one index you tried and dropped, because the plan didn't use it or it didn't pay. Say why, with its numbers. Week 11's worked example, where one index helped one query fifty times over and another not at all, is the kind of reasoning wanted.

In the report, a table for the three queries: the query in a line, the plan step and buffers before, the index, and the plan step and buffers after. Then one paragraph on what each index costs: its size from pg_relation_size, and which of your inserts and updates now have to maintain it.

Part B: one plan, step by step

Take the most interesting of the three, best one with a join, and explain its plan after your indexes, from the innermost step out:

  • What each step does, in a sentence, and why the planner chose it over the alternatives week 11 named (for a join: nested loop, hash join or merge join).
  • For each step, the rows the planner expected beside the rows it got. Where they differ ten times or more, follow week 12's checklist: ANALYZE, CREATE STATISTICS for related columns, a higher statistics target, or a rewrite. Say what you tried and what changed.
  • One thing that would make the planner choose differently: more rows, a different value, or an index you dropped.

Have this plan open at the walkthrough. You may be asked about any step of it.

Part C: two users at once, and a backup

Milestone 3's transaction kept a business rule for one user. Week 13 and Lab 8 showed what goes wrong when two users run it at the same moment.

  • Test it with two sessions. As in Lab 8, this needs PostgreSQL installed on your own computer, not Colab. In two psql windows, run your transaction's statements interleaved so that both pass the rule's check before either commits. Record both windows in m4_two_sessions.txt, marking the order of the steps. Does the rule still hold afterwards?
  • Choose a fix and show it working. Either lock what the check reads (SELECT … FOR UPDATE) or run the transaction at SERIALIZABLE. Rerun the same interleaving and record it too. In the report, say which you chose and why, and what it costs your users.
  • Retry in the program. If your transaction can fail with a serialization failure or a deadlock, the program retries it a few times before telling the user, as on week 13's slide on retrying (psycopg raises errors.SerializationFailure).
  • Restore a backup. Back up your database with pg_dump, as in week 14, restore it into a new, empty database, and check that each table's row count matches. Put the three or four commands and the counts in the report.

Part D: CS 5200 only

  • The NoSQL feature, finished and argued. Your jsonb part from milestones 2 and 3, with an index that serves its search: a GIN index or an index on an expression, measured before and after like Part A. In the report, about a page: why this part of your data suits a document, what you embedded and what you referenced, and what it costs, in week 15's terms (fixed shape, joins, keys, updates). Questions people usually ask

  • My queries are already fast. What do I write? Fast at 1,000 rows may not be at 100,000. Measure the buffers, not the milliseconds, and say what the plan would do with more rows. You may load more invented rows to show it.
  • The planner ignores my new index. That is a finding: run ANALYZE, check the plan again, and if it still isn't used, it may be your dropped index. Say why.
  • My transaction can't break with two users. Show the interleaving that would be the worst case and explain why it is safe, with the isolation level and the locks it takes.
  • Can I change my program after the report is in? You may fix bugs before the demo. Say what you changed at the start of the walkthrough.
