Term project: a database application of your own

Over the semester you design a database for a domain you choose, build it in PostgreSQL, put a small Python application on top of it, and then make it fast and explain how. Each milestone uses what the weeks before it taught.

Choosing a domain

Pick something you know well enough to ask good questions about: a club you belong to, a job you have had, a hobby. It should have at least six kinds of things worth storing and real rules about how they relate. A few that work well: a community food pantry, a climbing gym, a used car lot, a youth sports league, a recording studio's bookings, a small veterinary clinic.

The university and the library are off limits, because the course uses them as examples. If you are unsure whether a domain is big enough, bring it to office hours in week 6.

Working alone or in a pair

  • A team has one or two people. Tell me by the end of week 6 if you are working with a partner; you keep the same team all semester.
  • A team uploads one zip per milestone, named with both last names, and both partners get the same grade.
  • In the walkthrough each partner explains part of the code, and either may be asked about any of it.
  • A CS 4600 student and a CS 5200 student may work together. The team then does the graduate work as well.

CS 5200: the graduate part

Graduate students do two more things, graded inside the same 25 points:

  • A NoSQL feature: one part of your data stored and queried in a document or key-value form (for example PostgreSQL's jsonb, or a document store), with a short argument for why that part suits it. You sketch it in milestone 1 and build it by milestone 4.
  • A paper critique: two pages on a research paper about databases, chosen from the paper list by April 4 and due with milestone 4.

AI tools and the walkthrough

The course's limited-use AI policy applies: you may ask an AI tool to explain an error, a keyword or an idea, but the design, the SQL and the code must be your own. Every milestone ends with a short note saying how you used AI, if at all. The walkthrough in week 15 is where this matters: you explain your code line by line and answer questions about it, and a part you cannot explain earns no credit.

Late work

A milestone loses 10% per day for up to three days and is not accepted after that. The demo and walkthrough cannot be late: if you will miss April 27, tell me before then and we will find another time that week. Online students book a call in week 15 instead of demoing in class.
