Part B: nine questions in the algebra

Work in the playground's scratch pad. When an expression returns what the question asks for, copy it into lab1_answers.txt under its heading, with the number of tuples it returned. Symbols and words both work in the answers file.

  1. Q1. The name and salary of every instructor in the Comp. Sci. department.

  2. Q2. The titles of the courses offered by departments housed in Hale.

  3. Q3. The names of instructors who earn more than 85,000 and work in a department whose budget is under 100,000.

  4. Q4. The names of students who took a course in Fall 2025 and also a course in Spring 2026.

  5. Q5. The departments where no instructor earns less than 80,000.

  6. Q6. Each student's name with the title of every course they took outside their own department. Natural join will not do this one; use a theta join.

  7. Q7. Every pair of different students in the same department, each pair listed once. You will need ρ, as on slide 10.

  8. Q8. Run π[name](instructor ⋈ student). (a) In two or three sentences, say how many tuples it returns and why, naming the attributes the join matches on. (b) Write an expression that pairs each instructor's name with the name of every student in the same department.

  9. Q9a (everyone). The names of students who took every 4-credit course offered by the Comp. Sci. department, using ÷.

  10. Q9b (CS 5200 only). The same question without ÷, using only σ, π, ×, − and joins. Slide 19 shows the rewrite.

Check your counts against each other: Q4 needs an ∩, Q5 a −, and Q4, Q5 and Q9 each have between 2 and 4 tuples. If an expression returns 0 tuples, look at its tree in the playground to see which step empties it.

Write Q1 to Q8(b) in SQL in lab1.sql, and Q9b too if you are in CS 5200. Each query should return the same number of tuples as your algebra. Try them in the playground's SQL scratch pad first, then run the whole file in your own PostgreSQL:

If something goes wrong

  • "There is no attribute …" in the playground: an earlier π dropped that attribute, or two relations share the name and you need relation.attribute. The message lists the attributes that are there.
  • Fewer tuples than you expect from a natural join: the two sides share more attribute names than you meant. Slide 17 and example 3.4 show the fix.
  • Your SQL returns more rows than your algebra: SQL keeps duplicates. Add DISTINCT.
  • "each UNION query must have the same number of columns" or "types … cannot be matched": the two sides are not union-compatible (slide 13).
  • Anything else: copy the exact message and bring it to office hours, Tuesday and Wednesday 12:00–3:00 p.m., MIC room D159, or post it in the week 2 discussion in Brightspace.
